Month Boundary Scheduling

What was wrong with how the solver handled Dec 1 and Jan 1, what changed, and what it does now for every month of the year.

The convention the solver now follows

For November and December the department's request template carries the next month's first day as a trailing column, and that day is scheduled with the month whose sheet holds it. November keeps its own Nov 1 and gains Dec 1; December has no Dec 1 column and gains Jan 1; January has no Jan 1 column and runs from Jan 2. February and every other month run from the 1st to the last day as normal.

Nothing about this is hard-coded to those months. The solver looks at what the request sheets actually contain. A sheet whose date row ends with a wrapped "1" extends the schedule by that day; a sheet with no column for its own day 1 leaves that day to the previous month. A February template has a Feb 1 column and no trailing day, so February is untouched.

What was wrong

Two problems, found while checking how cpsatv2-jan2 managed to staff Jan 1 when no January sheet offers it.

  1. The importer invented Jan 1 availability. The January template's date row runs 2 to 31. For a day missing from that row, the importer fell back to a fixed column offset, and the offset for day 1 is column B, which on this template is Jan 2. All 78 January submissions came in with Jan 1 identical to Jan 2. Thirty physicians looked available on a day nobody had offered, and the solver filled all 21 slots from that. December had the mirror problem: its trailing Jan 1 column was being read as Dec 1.
  2. The solver could only work a calendar month. Even with the right availability, a December solve stopped at Dec 31. The Jan 1 preferences live only on the December sheets, so there was no way to staff Jan 1 from them.

What changed

  1. Importer. The date row is split into runs of increasing day numbers. The longest run is the month. A day with no column is simply not offered. A trailing run that wraps to a small number is the next month's leading day, stored on the submission as spill-over availability dated in that next month. Verified on real files: Jan 1 availability in the January folder went from 30 physicians to 0; all 74 December files carry a Jan 1 column and 45 physicians offered it.
  2. Solver range. A solve now extends past the end of its month, one contiguous day at a time, for as long as the submissions carry those days. Availability and anchor-shift accounting include them. A December solve produces Dec 2 to Jan 1 with the result still filed under December. Opt-out exists for tests.
  3. Start day. When the previous month's schedule already staffs the leading day(s) of the month being solved, the solve starts after them and treats them as already worked, so the rest and consecutive-shift rules see Jan 1 exactly as they would see Dec 31. This works whether the December schedule came from the human master sheet or from the solver.
  4. Everything downstream follows the extra day. The Excel export writes it in the last week row, the department's own layout. Re-importing that export resolves it to the right date rather than mistaking it for day 1 of the month. Loading a saved schedule shows it on the grid and lets you edit it. The master-sheet push writes it into that month's own sheet. On-call assignment covers it, and skips any leading day the solve did not schedule.
  5. Previous-month panel. When you load December as the previous month before a January solve, the panel reports "Jan 1 already staffed on this sheet (23 shifts), the next month will be solved from day 2."

Verification

  • Backend test suite: 125 tests passing. The new ones cover a December solve staffing Jan 1 only for physicians who offered it, a November solve staffing Dec 1, the opt-out, an export-then-reimport round trip that keeps Jan 1 dated in 2027, on-call on the extra day, and the start-day logic for a solver-made December.
  • Real data: the importer fix was checked against every January, December and November submission on this machine. November: 77 of 80 sheets label days 1 to 30 and then Dec 1; 42 physicians offer Nov 1 and 35 offer Dec 1. Last night's November solve staffed Nov 1 from those genuine Nov 1 preferences; what the old importer lost was the Dec 1 column, which the solver can now schedule.
  • Not yet exercised: a full real December solve on the new code. The first one is worth a look at the Jan 1 column of the export, and at Jan 2 and Jan 3 after a January solve that follows it.

KEAsked · Scheduler Rulebook

Every rule the scheduler follows

A plain-English map of everything the CP-SAT solver considers when it builds a month's schedule — what it will never break, what it balances against everything else, and which per-physician flags change its behaviour. For physicians and schedulers, not programmers.

Hard rule

Never violated. The solver would rather leave a shift completely unfilled than break one of these — they define what a legal schedule even is.

Soft rule

A preference, not a law. The solver weighs it against every other soft rule and generally honours it — but will trade it away under enough pressure from more important things.

Soft-rule priority scale

Every soft rule below is tagged with one of these five tiers, based on its actual weight in the solver's scoring. Higher tiers almost always win when two soft rules conflict.

Coverage & assignment basics

4 rules

The foundation every schedule sits on: who can be where, and when.

HC-1

One physician per slot

Hard

Exactly one person can hold any given shift slot on any given day. Two physicians can never be double-booked into the same slot.

HC-2

One shift per physician per day

Hard

No physician works more than one shift in a single day, no matter how many they're available for.

HC-3 – HC-6

Only where you said you're available

Hard

A physician is only ever assigned to a day, time block, and site they actually marked as available in their monthly submission. A stray checked box on a day you didn't mark as a working day doesn't count as an offer to work it.

HC-7

Your monthly shift cap

Hard

Nobody is ever scheduled past their own maximum shift count for the month. Casual physicians are capped at exactly their requested count, never their max — see Fairness below for why.

Workload limits

5 rules

How much, and how often, one physician can be stacked up in a row.

HC-8

Consecutive-day cap (SIAR)

Hard

Across any run of days, a physician never works more than their own personal maximum-consecutive-shifts setting (3 by default, higher for some physicians). This carries across a month boundary too — day 1 of a new month knows what you worked on the last days of the previous one.

HC-13

Consecutive-night cap (NIAR)

Hard

The same idea, specifically for 2400h night shifts: nobody works more nights in a row than their own max-consecutive-nights setting. Also carries across month boundaries.

HC-12

1800h-shift run limit

Hard

For physicians with a lowered personal limit, no more than a set number of 1800h shifts in a row (default allows an effectively unlimited run; only a handful of physicians have a real cap set).

HC-12c

Same-site run limit

Hard

"No two Intake shifts in a row," generalized: a physician with this limit set can't work more than N consecutive days at the one site, whichever site it is.

4-in-a-row discouraged

Soft

Working 4 days straight is legal for anyone whose personal cap allows it — but the solver still mildly discourages it as a last resort, for anyone it's allowed for at all.

weight 35 · Medium

Rest & safety

8 rules

Minimum recovery time between shifts, and protection against exhausting back-to-back patterns.

HC-9

23-hour minimum rest

Hard

At least 23 hours must separate the start times of any two of your shifts on consecutive days. If the gap would be shorter, one of the two shifts simply can't be assigned to you.

HC-9

Extended rest after a late shift

Hard

A shift starting at 2000h or later (including 2400h nights) blocks the entire following day outright, and still requires a noon-or-later start the day after that — unless the day in between is itself part of the same continuing night run.

Avoid a jarring time-of-day swing

Soft

Even when the rest gap is technically fine, the solver discourages a big jump in start time between two consecutive working days — say, 0600h one day and 1800h the next — since that's disruptive in a way the pure rest-hours check doesn't capture.

weight 35 · Medium

Avoid an unusually long gap

Soft

A gap of more than 36 hours between two shifts (e.g. a 0600h shift followed by a 2400h the next night) is technically allowed, but strongly discouraged — the solver only accepts it when nothing better is available.

weight 300 · Very High

Mandatory day off after a late shift

Hard

For physicians with the "late-shift rest privilege" flag set, any 1600h, 1800h, or 2000h shift forces the entire next day off, no exceptions.

Post-block cool-down

Hard

For physicians with this flag set, finishing a work stretch of a certain minimum length triggers a fixed number of fully protected days off afterward before they can be scheduled again — the single most-requested new rule from the last preferences survey ("48 hours off after nights is essential").

No isolated 2400h nights

Hard

Unless a physician specifically prefers isolated nights (see Flags), every 2400h shift must have an adjacent-day 2400h shift next to it — a lone midnight shift surrounded by days off isn't allowed. This is also what makes "every night worked is part of a run of 2+" happen automatically.

Never night / day-off / night

Hard

For every physician, including those who prefer isolated nights: two 2400h shifts with exactly one empty day between them is never allowed. A single isolated night is fine for them; a "night, gap, night" pattern is not, for anyone.

Anchor shifts (0600h & 2400h)

5 rules

Early starts and midnight shifts get their own layer of rules, since they're the least popular and hardest to staff fairly.

Honour your own anchor request

Soft

If you specifically said how many 0600h or 2400h shifts you want, the solver actively tries to give you that many of that specific type — not just count toward your total shift number some other way.

weight 90 · High

Don't go far over your anchor request

Soft

Your count of each anchor type is capped at your request plus a small tolerance (1, by default). Going even that far over carries a real cost, so it only happens when nothing else can fill the slot — and when someone has to absorb overage, whoever's own numbers lean that direction absorbs it first.

weight 500 (150 if it's your stated direction) · Very High

Fallback night-shift ceiling

Soft

If you never stated a 2400h preference at all (not even a zero), the solver still keeps you under a flat fallback ceiling — night-shift burden is a fairness concern serious enough not to leave completely open on a blank field. No equivalent ceiling exists for 0600h, since early starts don't carry the same wellbeing concern.

Configurable · scheduler_config.yaml

Nobody who asked is left at zero

Soft

If you explicitly requested some 0600h or 2400h shifts, the solver guarantees — as close to a hard rule as a soft preference gets — that you're not left with none of that type while someone who never asked gets some, as long as it's genuinely achievable.

Guaranteed order, not a plain weight

Minimum anchor floor for 0/0 requests

Soft

New. If you explicitly say you want zero 0600h and zero 2400h shifts, the solver still nudges you toward at least 1 anchor shift (2 if you're requesting 10 or more shifts overall) — so anchor coverage doesn't fall entirely on whoever happened to ask for it. Never forced if you genuinely have no anchor-type availability that month.

weight 400 · Very High
Not yet validated on a real multi-physician solve

Fairness & honouring requests

9 rules

Making sure everyone gets close to what they asked for, and that nobody's request structurally crowds out someone else's.

Reach your requested count first

Soft

Closing the first half of the gap between what you're currently scheduled and what you requested is worth more per shift than topping up the second half — so scarce slots go to closing someone's real shortfall rather than spreading thin across people who are already close to their number.

weight 100×priority (first half), 50×priority (second half) · High / Medium

Your own stated picks, elevated

Soft

For physicians with "honour all requests" set, the specific date+shift you personally marked as your pick (not just anything you left available) gets a much bigger push than an ordinary available cell.

weight 150 vs 5 · High / Low
Applies to: physicians with honor_all_requests

Seniority weighting

Soft

New. A physician's hire year adds a small multiplier to how much their own requested-count bonus is worth — up to 30% more pull, growing 2% per year of tenure by default. Nobody with an unrecorded hire year is penalised; the multiplier is simply 1× until it's set.

Configurable · scheduler_config.yaml

Priority weight

Soft

A manual multiplier on top of (and separate from) seniority, for a physician whose requested count should carry more institutional weight than the general population. Never applies to casual physicians.

Configurable per physician · physicians.yaml

Casual staffing protection

Soft

Every non-casual physician's own requested shift count is protected almost completely before casual physicians compete for anything — casuals then fill in only from what's left, plus a tiny sliver (at most 1 shift per non-casual physician) genuinely up for competition. Casuals can never exceed their own requested count regardless.

Guaranteed order, not a plain weight

Shared-position merging

Soft

New. Two physicians who split a single position (e.g. P52 & P53) each submit their own share of the request; the scheduler sums their shift counts, combines their marked availability, and schedules the total across the union of both — rather than silently treating one submission as the whole truth and ignoring the other.

Configurable per physician · combined_with

Your maximum is never below your request

Hard

New. If a submission states a maximum shift count lower than its own requested count (a data-entry slip), the maximum is automatically raised to match the request — so a physician's own stated cap can never accidentally shut out their own ask.

Slight incentive to fill toward your max

Soft

A small, flat per-shift nudge toward using your full available range, not just your bare requested minimum, once everything more important is already satisfied.

weight 3 · Low

Hardest-to-staff shifts filled first

Soft

When not every slot can be filled, the solver prioritises which ones get a person at all: 2400h nights first, then weekend shifts, then 0600h starts, ahead of an ordinary weekday daytime shift. This only decides what's left unfilled under real pressure — it doesn't change who ends up in a slot once it's going to be filled regardless.

base 1000, +100 to +600 by category · Critical

Site & variety rules

9 rules

Spreading work across sites and shift times so a month doesn't flatten into the same site or the same hour, over and over.

No repeated shift code on back-to-back days

Hard

The exact same time-and-site combination can never be assigned to you two days running.

Acute / non-acute balance (Group A/B)

Soft

Each physician's month aims for roughly 40% acute-site (RAH A/B) shifts and 60% non-acute (RAH I, NEHC, RAH F) — enforced by rewarding an A-then-B or B-then-A pattern on consecutive days and penalising two acute shifts in a row.

+30 toward the A target, +20 alternation, −40 A-then-A · Medium

Within-Group-B site preference

Soft

A physician can set a standing preference for NEHC, generic RAH, or specifically RAH F side within their non-acute allocation — a gentle tie-break between otherwise-equal options.

weight 6 · Low
Applies to: physicians with group_b_site_preference

Float-shift (RAH F) target

Soft

For a physician who specifically wants RAH F ("float") shifts, a stronger targeted push toward a set monthly count — needed because only one float slot exists per day across the whole roster, so the generic tie-break above isn't assertive enough on its own.

weight 35 · Medium
Applies to: physicians with float_shift_target

Cluster your night shifts

Soft

Two consecutive 2400h nights earn a bonus over two isolated ones — a run of nights is easier on a physician and the roster than scattered singletons.

weight 18 · Medium

Complete your full night run

Soft

For a physician who specifically wants their nights clustered into one long run rather than several short ones, a stronger bonus specifically for reaching their own full max-consecutive-nights length.

weight 45 · Medium
Applies to: physicians with prefer_clustered_nights

Prefer isolated nights, when asked

Soft

The opposite of the above: a physician who's flagged as preferring singleton nights is exempted from the "no isolated night" hard rule, and the solver actively discourages stacking their nights consecutively.

weight 400 · Very High
Applies to: physicians with prefer_singleton_nights

Reward consecutive working days generally

Soft

Beyond nights specifically, any two consecutive worked days (of any shift type) earn a small bonus — discouraging isolated single-day assignments scattered across the month.

weight 10 · Low

Don't settle into one start time

Soft

Two mechanisms stop a month from quietly becoming "the same start time, almost every shift": a same-time-two-days-running penalty, plus an overall monthly cap on how many shifts of one start time you can rack up, scaled to how much you requested overall. (2400h is excluded — nights have their own clustering logic above.)

−40 repeat, −60 monthly overage · Medium / High

Weekend cap

Soft

Each physician has an effective cap on how many distinct weekends they touch in a month, scaled to how much they requested. Going over is allowed under pressure but costly. A physician with "prefer weekends" set has this waived entirely.

weight 250 · Very High

Concentrate weekends, don't spread them thin

Soft

For a similar total number of weekend shifts, the solver prefers piling them onto fewer distinct weekends (one solid Fri/Sat/Sun stretch) over touching many weekends lightly. Applies to everyone except physicians with "prefer weekends" set, since that flag's whole point is the opposite.

weight 15 · Low

Prefer-weekends bonus & continuity

Soft

A physician who wants more weekend work gets an active per-shift push toward Friday/Saturday/Sunday shifts, plus a penalty for an awkward Friday-and-Sunday-but-not-Saturday gap within one weekend.

+80 per weekend shift, −220 for a Sat gap · High
Applies to: physicians with prefer_weekends

Avoid a protected weekday

Soft

A physician can mark one weekday (e.g. a standing admin day) to be avoided — a mild per-shift penalty on that day specifically. The older "avoid Mondays" flag still works as shorthand for this.

weight 5 · Low
Applies to: physicians with avoid_weekday / avoid_mondays

Named-pair & individual overrides

6 rules

These aren't general rules — each exists for one specific physician or pair, configured by name in scheduler_config.yaml.

Linked-rest pairs

Hard

Two physicians who split one position (P52 & P53 today) are treated as a single combined timeline for rest purposes: never both scheduled the same day, the same 23-hour/36-hour/swing rules apply across their combined sequence, and their combined consecutive-day run is capped the same way one person's own run is.

Conditional co-working

Soft

A named pair (today: P27 & P42) can never both work a weekend, and on a weekday where both work, exactly one of them must be on a specified shift time.

Timed separation

Hard

A named pair (today: P09 & P29) must keep a minimum hour gap between their shift start times whenever both work the same day, extended across adjacent days too, plus specific time combinations banned outright regardless of gap.

Forbidden precursor shift

Hard

For a specific physician (today: P28), certain shift times the day before a 2400h night are banned outright — only an early-enough shift, or nothing at all, may come immediately before a night for them.

Night-chain ramp-in

Soft

For a named physician (today: P92), starting a run of night shifts cold off a day completely off is discouraged; a smaller bonus rewards having a specific preferred shift time (e.g. 1800h) the day right before a night run begins.

−30 cold start, +15 preferred lead-in · Medium / Low

On-call placement preference

Soft

A physician can set whether their on-call (day/night-call) assignments should land right after their own last regular shift, specifically before an evening shift the next day, or kept apart from their own shifts entirely. Handled by a separate on-call assignment step, not the main shift solve.

Applies to: physicians with call_linkage set

Submission validation

4 checks

These run before scheduling, on each physician's monthly submission — they check whether a request is even complete enough to schedule, and have nothing to do with how the solver actually assigns shifts.

min_valid_days

At least 1.5 available days marked for every shift requested — a request for 10 shifts needs at least 15 days on offer.

min_valid_blocks

At least 4 available time blocks in total for every shift requested.

min_weekend_days

At least 0.6 available weekend days for every shift requested.

min_anchored_days

At least 0.5 available early/late ("anchored") days for every shift requested.

Each of the four can be individually lowered or switched off per physician via a rule_overrides entry in physicians.yaml, and physicians flagged special_provisions or casual have validation failures auto-resolved every month rather than needing a human to click through them — the underlying issue still shows in the Validate page's detail view, just already marked resolved.

Per-physician flags, in full

24 flags

Every standing override available in a physician's roster entry, in one place. Most physicians have none of these set and simply run on the defaults above.

forbidden_sites

Sites this physician can never be assigned to. Hard.

only_2400h

Only ever assigned 2400h night shifts. Hard.

only_0600h

Only ever assigned 0600h shifts. Hard.

forbidden_shift_times

A list of specific shift start times this physician is never assigned. Hard.

no_call

Exempt from on-call (day-call/night-call) assignment entirely. Enforced in the on-call step, not the main shift solve.

cap_at_requested

Hard-caps total shifts at the requested count rather than the submitted maximum — no "going beyond what I asked for," ever.

prefer_weekends

Waives the weekend cap entirely and actively pushes weekend shifts their way (see Named-pair & individual overrides above).

max_weekends

A manual, fixed weekend cap that overrides the usual proportional calculation — for a standing exception like someone who always works every weekend.

honor_all_requests

Elevates this physician's specifically-marked daily picks far above an ordinary available cell.

prefer_singleton_nights

Exempts them from the "no isolated night" rule and actively discourages stacking their nights consecutively.

prefer_clustered_nights

The opposite: rewards completing their full-length night run rather than several shorter ones.

rest_after_late_shift

Forces the entire next day off after any 1600h/1800h/2000h shift. Hard.

max_consecutive_1800h

A personal cap on consecutive 1800h shifts, when lowered below the default of 3.

post_block_rest_days / post_block_min_length

A mandatory multi-day cooldown after finishing a work stretch of a set minimum length. Hard.

max_consecutive_same_site

A personal cap on consecutive days at the one site. Hard.

avoid_weekday / avoid_mondays

A soft penalty on one protected weekday (e.g. a standing admin day).

anchor_preference

A standing override for which anchor type (0600h or 2400h) this physician absorbs overage in, when a month's own numbers don't make it obvious.

group_b_site_preference

A standing tie-break preference for NEHC, generic RAH, or RAH F specifically within the non-acute allocation.

float_shift_target

A specific monthly target count of RAH F (float) shifts the solver actively pursues.

priority_weight

A manual multiplier on how much this physician's own requested count matters, relative to everyone else.

hire_year

New. Feeds the seniority multiplier — unset means no bonus at all, never treated as "hired this year."

combined_with

New. Marks this submission as one half of a shared position; its numbers and availability get merged into the named target physician and it's dropped from separate scheduling.

default_shifts_requested

Overrides a month's submitted requested count with a fixed standing number — for a physician whose monthly numbers are unreliable but who should still get normal priority every month.

special_provisions / casual

Auto-resolves this physician's monthly validation failures without a human needing to click through them. casual additionally deprioritizes them behind every non-casual physician's own requested count (see Fairness above).

Recently added or changed

From this session

  • Seniority weighting — a new hire_year field and multiplier, composing with the existing priority weight.
  • Shared-position merging (combined_with) — fixes a real case where two physicians splitting one position (P52 & P53) were being scheduled as if they were entirely unrelated, each against only their own half of the real combined demand.
  • Maximum-never-below-request floor — closes a data-entry trap where a stated maximum lower than the stated request silently shut out part of someone's own ask.
  • 0/0 anchor minimum floor — a physician who explicitly asks for zero of both anchor types still gets a soft nudge toward at least one. This is the least proven change here: a solo synthetic test needed a notably strong weight (400) before it had any effect at all, and it hasn't yet been checked against a real multi-physician solve.

Known inconsistencies worth knowing about

  • A handful of settings still live in scheduler_config.yaml — same_shift_consecutive, run_group_mix, linked_pairs, and the whole singleton block — that look configurable but are no longer read by the solver at all. The real behaviour they describe is hardcoded directly into the rules above (e.g. the same-shift-code rule and the Group A/B alternation reward); changing those YAML values today would do nothing.
  • The code's own internal rule numbering has a small duplicate: two unrelated hard rules are both labelled "HC-11" in the source (the anchor-shift limit, and the late-shift rest privilege). Purely a documentation slip in the code comments — it doesn't affect how either rule actually behaves.
Reflects the CP-SAT solver's logic as of this review. Weights and thresholds are tuned over time against real monthly solves — treat the numbers here as a snapshot, not a permanent contract.
